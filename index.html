Overview

The Smart Tactile Controller is a small USB-C macro pad: five mechanical keyswitches mapped to GPIO pins on an ESP32-C3, ready to trigger shortcuts, macros, or custom firmware behavior in whatever software you point it at.

It's a full hardware project end to end — schematic capture, footprint selection, PCB layout, and a 3D-printed enclosure — built to understand every layer between a switch press and a signal on a wire.

Schematic

USB-C feeds a linear regulator, which powers the ESP32-C3. Each switch lands on its own GPIO with the module's internal pull-up doing the work — no external pull-up resistors needed.

Build log

  1. 01

    Schematic & circuit design

    Captured the full circuit in KiCad — USB-C power input, LDO regulation, the ESP32-C3-MINI-1, and five switch nets, each referencing real datasheet pinouts rather than assumed ones.

  2. 02

    Debugging & validation

    Traced and fixed 221 malformed net connections across 17 footprints — missing net codes and mistyped power pins — until the design passed ERC and DRC clean.

  3. 03

    PCB layout

    Arranged the five switches in a 19.05 mm cross pattern, placed 0402 passives around the regulator and MCU, and brought the board down to a 58 × 85 mm footprint.

  4. 04

    Enclosure & fabrication

    Modeled a 3D-printed enclosure in Fusion 360 around the board's real STEP geometry, then sent Gerbers and drill files to PCBWay.
